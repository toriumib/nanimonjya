## A. 投稿カバーレター（英語）

*Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 向け。査読者はカバーレターも読む。誠実さが全て。

```
To the Editors,

I submit the manuscript "Determinism Without Fatalism: Making MacKay's
Logical Indeterminacy Measurable — the Self-Prediction Gap Minus Choice
Inconsistency" for consideration.

The manuscript makes no claim to conceptual novelty in its central
insight. As I state explicitly in §2, the core idea — that a
physically determinate agent can be unpredictable in principle to a
predictor whose prediction can reach the agent — is due to MacKay
(1960, Mind), and was independently developed on the computational
side by Lloyd (2012, Phil Trans R Soc A). My contribution is
methodological, not conceptual:

  (i)  a formal separation of determinism, predictability, and
       fatalism using interventionist causal semantics;
  (ii) an operationalization of MacKay's idea as the self-prediction
       gap Γ, with a deterministic, randomness-free instantiation;
  (iii) the observation that Γ alone is the wrong quantity, reducing
       in the near-indifference regime to choice inconsistency, and
       that the quantity isolating MacKay's mechanism is Γ − C;
  (iv) a preregisterable within-subject experimental design with
       falsification criteria.

I report no human data. The empirical program is specified but not
executed, and I state in the manuscript that I regard Γ − C = 0 as
the more likely outcome.

I have no conflicts of interest. I am an independent researcher
without institutional affiliation. The manuscript has not been
published or submitted elsewhere.

I would be glad to revise in response to review.

Sincerely,
Kengo Toriumi
toriumikengo@gmail.com
```

**注意点**:
- 「independent researcher without institutional affiliation」を明記する。査読者は身元が気になる。隠さない。
- 概念的新規性を主張しないのは、v18 の§2 と整合。査読者の信頼を得る。
- カバーレターに「MacKay の定量化」と書くのは §5 段階の指示通り。

### B-1. Aaron Schurger（Chapman University）— RP再解釈

```
Subject: Question about the readiness potential and deliberation depth

Dear Professor Schurger,

I am an independent researcher writing a manuscript on the logical
indeterminacy of self-prediction (building on MacKay 1960). Your
accumulator model of the readiness potential is central to my
argument (§7 of my manuscript), specifically the claim that the RP
is not a "decision signal" but an accumulation process.

I would be grateful for clarification on one point: whether, in the
accumulator framework, the RP amplitude reflects the AMOUNT of
planning/deliberation demanded by the task, rather than a fixed
pre-conscious decision. I understand Triggiani et al. (2026)
identify attention as the primary determinant of RP amplitude.

Would you agree with the reading that the RP corresponds to
"planning load" rather than "decision timing"?

Thank you for your time. No response is expected if you are busy.

Sincerely,
Kengo Toriumi
```

### B-2. Seth Lloyd（MIT）— 自由意志のチューリングテスト

```
Subject: Follow-up on "A Turing test for free will" (2012)

Dear Professor Lloyd,

Your 2012 paper in Phil Trans R Soc A is the second direct antecedent
of the manuscript I am writing (the first being MacKay 1960). I am
grateful to have found it, as it corrected my own overclaim of
novelty.

I am operationalizing your idea as the self-prediction gap Γ, and —
following a reviewer's point — subtracting baseline choice
inconsistency C, since Γ alone reduces to C in the near-indifference
regime.

I note in my manuscript that your proposed Turing test was never
implemented. Do you know of any attempt to implement it since 2012?

Sincerely,
Kengo Toriumi
```

### B-3. Uri Maoz（意識と自由意志の実験）

```

Dear Professor Maoz,

I am writing a manuscript that distinguishes the self-prediction gap
from baseline choice inconsistency (Γ − C). Your work on the neural
precursors of decisions is directly relevant to §7 of my manuscript.

I have one methodological question: in measuring the subjective
"feeling of free will," what do you regard as the minimum viable
single-item measure? I want to avoid inventing a poor instrument.

Sincerely,
Kengo Toriumi
```
