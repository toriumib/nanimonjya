How to Remember Names

Short guides written from the memory research, not from folklore.

Forgetting names is not a character flaw and it is not a sign that your memory is going. Names are a genuinely harder kind of material than almost anything else you learn about a person, for reasons that memory researchers have studied for decades. These guides explain why, and what to do instead.

Why you forget names instantly

You recognise the face but the name is gone. The reason is that a name is the one thing about a person that means nothing — and meaning is what memory runs on.

How to remember names at work

A five-step routine that fits into a real working day, plus what to do about video calls, group introductions and the colleague you have already met three times.

Spaced repetition for names

Cramming on the day you meet someone barely helps. Recalling the name after a gap is what makes it durable — and there is a simple schedule for it.

Names at parties and conferences

Twelve introductions in ten minutes is not a memory task you can win. How to win a smaller one, and how to salvage the names on the way home.

What the research actually says

The findings that hold up, the ones that get overstated, and the claims we deliberately do not make.
